I keep a grey notebook beside the kettle because that is where our morning handover usually happens. It is a modest system, but it catches the small decisions that otherwise disappear before lunch. The first page records the colour of the status labels. The second records the behaviour of the retry button. The third records who has to ring the support desk. Together, those pages give the team one place to check what we agreed and what happens next.

Last autumn, the team spent twenty minutes looking for a decision we had made the day before. The answer was in the centre of a chat thread: “Leave the warning grey until the supplier confirms.” Nobody had written down why. We eventually found the reason—the supplier’s API was still returning stale results—but by then the kettle had boiled twice. That is the entire case for the notebook. It does not make decisions better. It gives the next person somewhere to start.
