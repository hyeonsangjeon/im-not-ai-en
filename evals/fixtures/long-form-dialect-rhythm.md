# Long-form publication: dialect and rhythm

> Synthetic evaluation fixture. The notebook, team, supplier, API, events, and anecdote are fictional and were not derived from workplace data.

- Profile: `long-form`
- Register: `publication` with a lightly conversational British voice

## Source

~~~text
I keep a grey notebook beside the kettle because that is where our morning handover usually happens. It is a modest system, but it catches the small decisions that disappear before lunch. The first page records the colour of status labels. The second page records the behavior of the retry button. The third page records who has to ring the support desk.

At a high level, the notebook provides a number of important benefits. It provides visibility. It provides alignment. It provides continuity. In conclusion, these benefits make the notebook a valuable and transformative tool for the organisation.

Last autumn, the team spent twenty minutes looking for a decision we had made the day before. The answer was at the center of a chat thread: “Leave the warning grey until the supplier confirms.” Nobody had written down why. We found the reason eventually—the supplier’s API still returned stale results—but by then the kettle had boiled twice. That is the entire case for the notebook. It does not make decisions better. It gives the next person somewhere to start.
~~~

## Targeted copyedits

- Make `behavior` and `center` consistent with the passage's established British English.
- Compress the mechanical benefits sequence and canned conclusion without deleting the notebook's actual purpose.
- Leave the concrete kettle imagery, short sentences, and dry final qualification intact.

## Assertions

- **Fidelity:** Preserve the notebook's location and purpose, the three types of notes, the benefits claimed by the source, the search for a decision, the quoted warning, the stale API results, and the limited claim that the notebook provides a starting point rather than better decisions.
- **Naturalness:** Remove the corporate middle paragraph's mechanical repetition and mixed dialect while preserving the already natural opening and ending.
- **Register:** Read as a lightly conversational British workplace essay, not a management memo or product testimonial.
- **Voice preservation:** Keep the kettle motif, understated humour, British vocabulary and spelling, deliberate short sentences, and modest conclusion.
- **Non-invention:** Add no new notebook field, benefit, team practice, supplier detail, metric, result, or reason that the warning stayed grey.

## Common failures

- Convert the passage to US English or overlook the two accidental US spellings.
- Polish away the kettle, the dry ending, or the purposeful short sentences.
- Preserve the corporate middle paragraph while rewriting the already natural paragraphs.
- Claim that the notebook improves decisions or prevents mistakes.
